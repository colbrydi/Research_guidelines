# Requesting Letters of Reference and Recommendation

I am happy to serve as a reference or write recommendation letters for people I have worked with professionally.

Before requesting a letter, please consider whether I am the right person to write it. The strongest recommendation
letters come from shared professional experiences and specific observations that complement the rest of an application.
Employers, graduate programs, scholarship committees, and other reviewers can already see your resume, transcript, and
list of accomplishments. My role is to provide context about what it was like to work with you through examples such as
when I observed you:

- Overcome a challenge or setback
- Solve a difficult problem
- Take initiative beyond what was expected
- Demonstrate leadership
- Improve after receiving feedback
- Strengthen a team or resolve a conflict
- Make a meaningful contribution to a project, organization, or community
- Demonstrate professionalism and reliability

The more specific and personal these observations are, the stronger the letter will be.

I only write about experiences that I have personally observed. I do not include accomplishments, skills, or personal qualities
based solely on a resume or draft letter. I cannot discuss grades, GPA, or other academic records.

If you would like me to serve as a reference or write a recommendation letter, please send me:

1. A current resume or CV.
2. A draft recommendation letter.
   
The draft serves several purposes. It helps me remember experiences we shared that may be relevant to the opportunity, helps
me understand what role my letter should play within the larger application, and gives you practice thinking about what makes
an effective recommendation letter. It also helps identify accomplishments, contributions, or experiences that I may have
forgotten and provides me with a starting point for writing.

I will use the draft only as a guide. I will rewrite the letter in my own voice and only include stories and examples that I remember
and can honestly describe from firsthand experience. If I cannot verify a claim through my own experiences working with you, I
will not include it in the letter.

My goal is not simply to write a letter, but to write one that genuinely strengthens your application.
